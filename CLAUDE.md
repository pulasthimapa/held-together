# Held Together: working instructions

Channel: true stories of the people who built the world, told as a conversation between two
recurring illustrated engineers — **Elias**, an engineer of the period, and **Maya**, a
structural engineer of today. The evidence behind these rules is in `docs/research-knowledge.md`.

## Format
F1. Every episode is a conversation between Elias and Maya. Elias carries the story from
    inside the period. Maya asks what the viewer is thinking and brings the modern angle:
    what we now know, what we now measure, what the codes say today.
F2. **Elias is a composite character, not a real person, and is labelled as one on screen the
    first time he appears in an episode.** Never write dialogue for a named real individual.
    Real people (Cooper, McLure and the rest) are depicted and quoted from the record only.
F3. Elias and Maya appear in every episode and never change face. The rest of the troupe
    (Tom, Sam, Ruth, Walter) play whatever roles a story needs.
F4. Characters are on screen for roughly 90 seconds an episode: the open, the close, and
    three or four turning points. Their voices carry much more; diagrams and stills carry
    the middle. This is a cost ceiling as much as an editorial one.
F5. Tone follows the story. Sombre where people died, warmer for ingenuity and recovery.
    Never flippant about deaths.

## Stories and scripts
1. Only true stories. Every factual claim needs an opened source recorded in the episode's
   fact sheet. Never use a search snippet or AI summary as a source. When sources disagree,
   use the safer wording and note the disagreement in the description.
2. Emotion comes from real people and real documents (letters, telegrams, testimony),
   paraphrased and credited. No invented suffering, no graphic death imagery, no shock
   framing in titles.
3. No two episodes share a story skeleton. Vary the opening (the event, a document, the
   aftermath, an object), the structure and the ending. Only the two hosts stay fixed.
4. Both hosts are AI voices. **This is a known, accepted risk**: research findings 11 and 14
   say story audiences reject AI narration and reward visible human authorship, and the
   channel now has no human voice. Rule 5a is the early-warning system for it; take a
   trigger there seriously rather than explaining it away.
5. Write the title and thumbnail idea before the script. Drop a story that has no clear
   thumbnail moment.
5a. After each upload, count comments that mention AI, robots or fake voices. If they appear
    on two episodes in a row, stop and propose a fix, including putting a real human voice
    into the episode.
5b. Lock the story structure (beats and ending) before generating any images.
5c. End every episode with one open question that invites viewers to share their own version
    (research finding 15).
5d. Keep an authorship evidence folder per episode (fact sheet, script drafts, project
    files), dated, so a wrongful "inauthentic content" flag can be appealed with proof
    (finding 16).

## Cast and visuals
6. The troupe never plays members of a specific Indigenous or cultural community in costume.
   Those people appear through archival photos or respectful symbols.
7. Every shot moves. Engineering explainers are drawn in code as HTML/CSS motion graphics.
   No still-image slideshows.
7a. Change something visually at least every 5-7 seconds, and land a pattern interrupt every
    30-60 seconds (findings 18 and 19). Scenes carry two or three angles so the edit can cut
    inside them.
7b. Hero video clips are animated from our own character stills, so the cast performs rather
    than being pushed in on.

## Output and publishing
8. One long episode a week, plus vertical cuts. Never mass-upload.
9. Vertical cuts must be at least 61 seconds, so they qualify for TikTok rewards. Upload them
   natively to each platform, never with another platform's watermark.
10. Every YouTube upload ticks "Altered or synthetic content" and lists sources in the
    description.
11. Once a month, post one human behind-the-scenes item: a sketch, a source document, or
    Indeepa talking through a calculation. With rule 4 as it now stands, this is the only
    human signal the channel has, so it does not get skipped.

## Working rules for Claude
12. Separate facts, hypotheses and conclusions. Label single-source claims as unverified.
13. Before stating any platform rule or price, re-check it, as they change often. Re-check
    YouTube monetisation rules every quarter.
14. Keep monthly tool spend under £50 until revenue starts. Say so before any change that
    raises it, and give the per-episode figure.
